Ombelle breaks ground in Fort Lauderdale's Flagler Village

Drilling is underway at 300 NE 3rd Avenue, where Dependable Equities and ODA Architecture are building two 44-story towers holding 754 residences and more than 100,000 square feet of amenities.

By Jake Nicholas August 12, 2026

Fort Lauderdale is set to welcome one of its largest new residential projects. Drilling has begun at 300 NE 3rd Avenue, where Ombelle is rising as two 44-story towers holding 754 residences in the center of Flagler Village, a development spanning about 1.46 million square feet.

ODA Architecture, the New York firm behind boundary-pushing residential work across Brooklyn and Manhattan, is handling architecture, interiors, and landscape in-house. More than 11,000 square feet of retail and restaurant space activates the base along NE 3rd Avenue.

More than 100,000 square feet of amenities sit above the retail floors. Morning light crosses one pool deck while a second catches the sunset, and residents drift between ceramic-clad kitchens, a golf simulator, and the shock of a cold plunge. Pickleball courts, a podcast studio, and a pet spa round out the upper floors.

A 35,000-square-foot Equinox anchors the wellness side, the club's first location in Fort Lauderdale and in Broward County. The gym was announced ahead of groundbreaking.

"We're building for people who want to live, work, and recover in one place, not commute across the city for it," said a principal at Dependable Equities, the South Florida arm of New York based Sky Equity Group. The firm has been stacking a Fort Lauderdale pipeline with ODA as its design partner, including a sculptural 47-story tower planned at 633 SE Third Avenue and a 45-story mixed-use tower at 101 SE 7th Street along the New River.

Flagler Village is where much of downtown Fort Lauderdale's residential surge is landing. Broward County has drawn a steady stream of wealth and finance migration out of the Northeast, and the district's supply of new luxury towers has struggled to keep pace with demand. Ombelle joins a corridor filling fast, from FAT Village, the $500 million arts district taking shape on 5.6 acres, to the Viceroy-branded twin towers Naftali Group is planning nearby.

Completion is expected in 2028, by which point Flagler Village will look less like a warehouse fringe and more like the vertical heart of downtown Fort Lauderdale.
