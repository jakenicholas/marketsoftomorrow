Four new hotels reshape Ibiza this season, from a 3,000-year-old castle to a nature-led temple

Ibiza spent this season quietly rewriting what a stay on the island can be, and it took four very different hotels to do it. Together they trace the arc of where Ibiza is heading.

By Kait Nicholas July 15, 2026

Ibiza spent this season quietly rewriting what a stay on the island can be, and it took four very different hotels to do it. Together they trace the arc of where Ibiza is heading: beyond the club-season cliché and toward a year-round destination built on heritage, gastronomy, wellness, and thought-out design.

After nearly two decades of archaeological delays and a roughly €47 million rehabilitation, the Parador de Ibiza opened to its first guests on March 10, crowning the UNESCO-listed Dalt Vila. Operated by Spain's state-run Paradores network, it is the 99th Parador in the country and the first in the Balearic Islands, set inside the restored 16th-century Almudaina Castle reaching back 3,000 years, through Phoenician, Roman, Islamic, and Renaissance chapters of the island's history. Its 41 keys come with an outdoor pool suspended over the ancient city walls, a wellness spa, and sweeping views across the old town to the Mediterranean beyond. Dining centers on Almudaina, a restaurant offering Mediterranean and Ibizan cuisine with a modern twist under Chef Adrián García Soto, alongside Pati d'Armes, a historic café and courtyard serving breakfast, brunch, and gourmet light bites.

Besai Ibiza Boutique Hotel opened on June 1 on a pine-covered hillside in San José, a 17-room sanctuary devoted to slow living. Positioned to catch some of the island's most celebrated sunsets, it carries a design concept led by hotel and creative director Karen Sbardelotto that draws on Ibiza's Phoenician heritage through hand-finished detail and a soft, neutral palette. A generous indoor pool anchors the wellbeing offering alongside private yoga and massage, and the all-day restaurant runs a Mediterranean-inspired menu from breakfast through dinner, all within easy reach of Cala Conta and Cala Bassa on the calmer southwest coast.

BLESS Ibiza The Site opened in early June as the 461-room second Ibiza property from BLESS Collection Hotels, the contemporary-luxury brand from Palladium Hotel Group, with interiors by Lázaro Rosa-Violán Studio. Every room and suite opens onto its own private terrace framing sea or island views, and the most indulgent come with outdoor plunge pools, sun-drenched Balinese beds, and in-room cocktail service. But the real story is the food: BLESS is the beating heart of The Site Ibiza, a sprawling entertainment-and-dining destination on Playa d'en Bossa that pulls the biggest names in global gastronomy onto a single stretch of coast, gathering Hell's Kitchen by Gordon Ramsay, StreetXO by Dabiz Muñoz, COYA, Leña and Lobito de Mar by Dani García, the multi-sensory Sublimotion, and TATEL Ibiza. Alongside the earlier The Unexpected Ibiza Hotel and the art-and-fashion Ibiza Gallery, it completes The Site's turn into one of the Mediterranean's most ambitious luxury developments.

On the rugged coast of Portinatx, Nômade Temple Ibiza opened on July 1 as the brand's second Spanish destination and a defining evolution of its Temple concept. Following acclaimed outposts in Tulum and Holbox, the 182-key retreat is conceived to give the land back to nature, its architecture by Nômade's in-house Oneness studio, co-founded by Danish architect Bjarke Ingels, sculpted into the cliffs and pine using piedra blanca, sabina wood, and terracotta tiles. Accommodations range from rooms and junior suites to lofts, ocean-front suites, and private villas, many with terraces, and private pools. Anchored by a subterranean spa inspired by the island's cave systems, water therapies lead a ritual program of yoga, meditation, and sound healing, all complimentary for in-house guests. Dining unfolds across a cluster of distinct venues: the fire-led La Popular, the seaside 4FU3GOS, the intimate Kuu Jū omakase counter, and the all-day Café Libre for coffee, smoothies, and island cheeses, while Dante's HiFi brings a Japanese-inspired listening-bar speakeasy of vinyl and cocktails after dark.

Four hotels, four philosophies, one island proving it holds all of them at once. From a castle 3,000 years in the making to a temple built to disappear into the landscape, Ibiza's newest addresses map a season that will be hard to top.
