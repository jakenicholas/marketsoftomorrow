Inside 'Alohilani Resort Waikiki Beach, the Oahu resort built on Queen Lili'uokalani's royal beachfront

Alohilani Resort Waikiki Beach rises on the Kalakaua Avenue shoreline where Queen Lili'uokalani kept her private retreat, with 839 Rockwell Group rooms, a three-story saltwater Oceanarium, and Morimoto dining.

By Jake Nicholas August 17, 2026

Waikiki's resort row is stacked with marquee names, but one sits on ground a queen once called her own. 'Alohilani Resort Waikiki Beach anchors the Kalakaua Avenue shoreline directly across from Kuhio Beach, Diamond Head filling the horizon, and its story starts long before check-in.

Guests here are walking the exact beachfront where Queen Lili'uokalani kept her private retreat. She called it Ke'alohilani, "the royal brightness." The last reigning monarch of the Hawaiian Kingdom, a prolific composer and author, relaxed and welcomed guests on this stretch of shoreline, and the resort carries her name and her welcome forward.

Arrival sets the tone. Two towers rise 39 floors, holding 839 rooms and suites with interiors by Rockwell Group, every one opening onto a private lanai facing the ocean, Diamond Head, or the city. The whole property is the product of a $125 million redevelopment that reopened the former Pacific Beach Hotel in May 2018.

The entrance is one of the grandest in all of Waikiki. Valets meet cars at the drive, the open-air arrival court flows between the towers, and the indoor lobby delivers the showstopper. Behind the check-in desk, a 280,000-gallon saltwater Oceanarium climbs three stories, home to more than 1,000 protected marine species. Divers descend twice daily for feedings, around 10:30 a.m. and 2:30 p.m. The lobby bar fills for the show.

Five floors up, a 17,000-square-foot seascape deck spreads toward the Pacific, anchored by a heated saltwater infinity pool named among America's most Instagram-worthy. Private cabanas hover over a reflecting pool. Tiered day beds and driftwood sculptures frame the water, fire pits flicker at dusk, and a heated keiki pool keeps families in the mix.

Iron Chef Masaharu Morimoto runs two restaurants on property. Morimoto Asia Waikiki pairs Asian-Western fine dining with indoor-outdoor seating and ocean views. Momosan goes loose and loud with ramen, sushi, sake, and craft beer. SWELL Restaurant & Pool Bar handles the deck, keeping swimmers fed without ever leaving the water's edge.

Days here stretch easily between a lomi-style unwind at Spa U'ilani, where massage and facial treatments draw on island traditions, a session at FIT Waikiki, and a few sets on the tennis and pickleball courts upstairs. On the lobby level, the Hawaiian Cultural Center hosts daily lei making, hula lessons, and Hawaiian history. Billed as a carbon-neutral property on Oahu, the resort is also returning 100,000 indigenous trees to the islands with the Hawaiian Legacy Reforestation Initiative, planted as the 'Alohilani Forest at Gunstock Ranch on the North Shore.

This fall, the resort throws the queen's legacy wide open. September 2 brings the Queen's Jubilee, a full day honoring Lili'uokalani on the ground of her former home. The Royal Gathering fills the property with live Hawaiian music, hula, and artisan experiences. The Queen's Garden Tea steeps an immersive celebration of Hawaii's flowers, fragrances, and flavors. A limited-time Royal Lomi Lomi Massage runs at Spa U'ilani, and SWELL pours queen-inspired mocktails and cocktails with special entertainment.

Then the Hawai'i Food & Wine Festival takes over from October 16 through November 8, with 'Alohilani and its Maui sister, Royal Lahaina Resort & Bungalows, both serving as host venues. On Kaanapali Beach, Royal Lahaina stages "No EGGScuses, Let's Brunch!" on October 25, six acclaimed chefs riffing on the egg against the Maui shoreline. 'Alohilani's signature turn comes November 6 with 24K Magic, an evening gathering acclaimed chefs, mixologists, premium wines, and island flavors on the same royal beachfront where Lili'uokalani once welcomed guests of her own.
