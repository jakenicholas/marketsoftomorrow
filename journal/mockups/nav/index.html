Four ways to make it a button

The count is the point. A number next to your face means things you track moved — that is a reason to click, where a gear icon never is. Each option below merges the two controls so the corner has one job.

JNDashboard3 Recommended

A · One merged button

Face, label and count in a single pill. It is unmistakably a button, it says where it goes, and the number carries the urgency the Pulse pill used to. Replaces both controls. Costs the most horizontal room.

My Dashboard No count

B · Labelled with a live dot

Green pill in the brand's action colour. The pulsing dot signals "something is new" without a number, so it never looks stale. Reads clearly as a destination, but loses the specificity of "3 moved".

JN3 Most compact

C · Avatar with a count badge

The familiar pattern from every app people already use. Smallest footprint, keeps the corner clean. The weakness: it still looks like an account control, so the discovery problem only half goes away.

Pulse10 JNMine3 Keeps both

D · Segmented: market news vs my news

Keeps Pulse (what moved everywhere) and adds Mine (what moved in my book) as one control. The most honest information design of the four, because those two numbers genuinely mean different things. Widest option.

Explore: ask first, then pick the view

The database isn't the map — the map is one view of it. So is the Atlas, so is a market page, so is a firm page. Explore should ask what you're looking for, then offer the lens.

The shift: today someone must know that "Atlas" means leaderboards before they can use it. Here they say what they want and we offer how to look at it — which is what a search engine does, and what turns nine features into one surface.
